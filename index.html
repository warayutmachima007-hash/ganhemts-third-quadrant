GaN HEMT third-quadrant conduction: how the drain falls below the gate

A GaN HEMT has no body diode, yet it carries reverse current during dead time. The gate never rises. The inductor pulls the drain below ground until the gate sits Vth above it, and the channel turns on from the drain side.

The governing equation

VSD = Vth + |VGS(off)| + IL·RDS(on)

The channel opens when VGD = VG − VD reaches Vth. With the gate held at VGS(off), the drain must fall to VGS(off) − Vth − I·R. The device behaves like a diode-connected FET: a deeper drain opens the channel further, which holds the drain at that level.

Design implications

  1. Zero reverse recovery. Conduction is unipolar, so no minority charge is stored. This is why GaN suits hard-commutated legs such as CCM totem-pole PFC.
  2. Dead-time loss is higher than a Si body diode. P ≈ 2·VSD·IL·tdead·fsw. Keep dead time short or adaptive.
  3. Negative gate bias costs volts. Each volt of off-bias adds one volt to VSD. Use the least negative bias the layout's dv/dt immunity allows.
  4. COSS sets the timing, not the depth. The node falls at dv/dt = IL/Cnode. How far below ground it settles is set by VSD alone.
